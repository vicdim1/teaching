# Design Thinking Framework: Tutor CRM Discovery

A structured approach to understanding your wife's tutoring workflow and identifying the real problems to solve.

## Framework Overview

This framework follows the Design Thinking process:
1. **Empathize** - Understand the user deeply
2. **Define** - Identify the core problems
3. **Ideate** - Explore potential solutions
4. **Validate** - Test assumptions
5. **Prioritize** - Focus on what matters most

---

## Phase 1: Empathize - Understanding Current Workflow

### Goal
Understand how she currently manages her tutoring business, without assuming what she needs.

### A. Daily/Weekly Workflow Questions

**Ask these to understand her routine:**

1. **Morning Routine**
   - "Walk me through your typical morning before teaching. What do you check or prepare?"
   - "How do you know who you're teaching today and when?"
   - "What materials or information do you need to gather before a session?"

2. **During Teaching**
   - "What information do you typically want to capture during or right after a session?"
   - "Do you take notes? Where and how?"
   - "What do you wish you remembered to write down but often forget?"

3. **End of Day/Week**
   - "What admin work do you do at the end of the day?"
   - "How much time do you spend on non-teaching tasks per week?"
   - "What tasks feel most tedious or time-consuming?"

4. **Month End**
   - "Walk me through how you create an invoice from start to finish."
   - "How long does it take to do invoices for all 20 students?"
   - "What's the most frustrating part of the invoicing process?"

### B. Pain Points Discovery

**Listen for emotional cues while asking:**

5. **Frustration Points**
   - "What part of managing your students makes you go 'ugh, I have to do this again'?"
   - "If you could eliminate one task from your week, what would it be?"
   - "What keeps you up at night about your tutoring business?"
   - "Have you ever forgotten something important? What happened?"

6. **Time Wasters**
   - "Where do you feel you waste the most time in your admin work?"
   - "What task takes longer than it should?"
   - "How often do you find yourself searching for information you know you wrote down somewhere?"

7. **Error-Prone Areas**
   - "Where do mistakes most commonly happen?"
   - "Have you ever billed incorrectly? What caused it?"
   - "Do you ever lose track of sessions or forget to bill for them?"

### C. Current Tools & Systems

**Understand what she's already using:**

8. **Excel Sheet Questions**
   - "Show me your Excel sheet. How is it organized?"
   - "What information do you track for each student?"
   - "What do you love about using Excel? What frustrates you?"
   - "How do you update it? Daily? Weekly? After each session?"

9. **Other Tools**
   - "Besides Excel, what other tools do you use? (Calendar, notes app, etc.)"
   - "How do these tools work together? Do you duplicate information anywhere?"
   - "Is there a tool you tried but stopped using? Why?"

10. **Student Work Analysis**
    - "Tell me about when you upload student work to ChatGPT. How often do you do this?"
    - "What specific insights are you looking for from the AI?"
    - "What do you do with the AI's feedback? How do you share it?"
    - "Is this process helpful? What could make it better?"

---

## Phase 2: Define - Identifying Core Problems

### Goal
Move from observations to problem statements.

### A. Ranking Pain Points

**Ask her to rank these:**

11. **Priority Exercise**
    - "If we could only solve ONE problem for you, what would have the biggest impact on your life?"
    - "What would save you the most time each week?"
    - "What would reduce your stress the most?"
    - "What would help you provide better service to your students?"

### B. Jobs-to-be-Done

**Understand the underlying goals:**

12. **What are you trying to achieve?**
    - "When you track a session in Excel, what are you really trying to accomplish?"
    - "What does a 'successful month' look like for you?"
    - "What information do parents/students expect from you?"
    - "How do you track student progress over time?"

13. **Success Metrics**
    - "How do you know if you're doing a good job managing your business?"
    - "What would 'organized and stress-free' look like for your tutoring work?"

---

## Phase 3: Ideate - Exploring Solutions

### Goal
Understand what solutions would actually help, in her words.

### A. Dream Scenario

**Ask aspirational questions:**

14. **Ideal World**
    - "If you had a magic wand, how would you manage your tutoring business?"
    - "What would your perfect day look like, admin-wise?"
    - "If money and tech skills were no object, what would you want?"

15. **Feature Wishlist**
    - "What would make invoicing effortless for you?"
    - "How would you want to track student progress?"
    - "What information would you want at your fingertips before each session?"

### B. Must-Haves vs Nice-to-Haves

**Separate essentials from extras:**

16. **Critical Features**
    - "What features would you use every single day?"
    - "What would you be willing to pay for?"
    - "If the system was missing X, would you still use it?"

17. **Workflow Preferences**
    - "Would you rather log sessions on your phone or computer?"
    - "Do you want reminders/notifications, or would that be annoying?"
    - "How much automation vs manual control do you want?"

---

## Phase 4: Validate - Testing Assumptions

### Goal
Challenge your assumptions about what she needs.

### A. Current Solution Validation

**Test assumptions about the CRM we built:**

18. **Feature Validation**
    - "I'm thinking of building a student database. What information would you want to track for each student?"
    - "For invoicing, would you want it to auto-generate monthly, or would you prefer to trigger it manually?"
    - "Would you want the AI work analysis integrated, or do you prefer using ChatGPT separately?"

19. **Workflow Validation**
    - "Would you log sessions immediately after teaching, or in batches at the end of the day/week?"
    - "Who would send the invoices - you or the system automatically?"
    - "How important is it to track which sessions have been invoiced vs. not yet billed?"

### B. Barriers & Concerns

**Understand potential obstacles:**

20. **Adoption Barriers**
    - "What would prevent you from using a new system?"
    - "How much time can you invest in learning something new?"
    - "Would you want to migrate data from your current Excel, or start fresh?"

21. **Privacy & Control**
    - "Are you comfortable with student data in the cloud vs. on your computer?"
    - "Do you need to access this from your phone, or mainly from your laptop?"
    - "Would you want to export/backup your data regularly?"

---

## Phase 5: Prioritize - Focus on Impact

### Goal
Determine what to build first based on impact vs. effort.

### A. Impact Assessment

**Quantify the benefits:**

22. **Time Savings**
    - "How much time do you currently spend on:"
      - Tracking sessions?
      - Calculating monthly hours?
      - Creating invoices?
      - Analyzing student work?
    - "If we could save you 5 hours per month, where would you want those savings?"

23. **Money Impact**
    - "Have you ever failed to bill for a session? How often?"
    - "Do late invoices affect when you get paid?"
    - "Would better organization help you take on more students?"

### B. Quick Wins vs. Long-term Goals

**Separate immediate needs from future enhancements:**

24. **Phase 1: MVP (What to build first)**
    - "If you could only have 3 features working next week, what would they be?"
    - "What would make you want to switch from Excel immediately?"
    - "What would give you the fastest relief?"

25. **Phase 2: Enhancement (What can wait)**
    - "What features would be nice but you could live without for now?"
    - "What would you want added after using the basic system for a month?"

## Interview Tips

### How to Conduct the Interview

**Do's:**
- ✅ Ask open-ended questions ("How do you..." not "Do you...")
- ✅ Listen more than you talk (80/20 rule)
- ✅ Ask "why" 5 times to get to root causes
- ✅ Observe her actually doing the tasks (ask her to show you)
- ✅ Take notes without interrupting
- ✅ Ask for stories/examples, not hypotheticals
- ✅ Validate feelings ("That sounds frustrating...")

**Don'ts:**
- ❌ Don't suggest solutions during empathy phase
- ❌ Don't defend the current system
- ❌ Don't assume you know what she needs
- ❌ Don't ask leading questions ("Wouldn't it be great if...")
- ❌ Don't try to cover everything in one session

### Interview Structure Suggestion

**Session 1: Understanding Current State (45-60 min)**
- Questions 1-10 (workflow and current tools)
- Goal: Deep understanding without any solution talk

**Session 2: Pain Points & Priorities (30-45 min)**
- Questions 11-17 (problems and ideals)
- Goal: Identify what matters most

**Session 3: Solution Validation (30 min)**
- Questions 18-25 (validate the CRM concept)
- Goal: Confirm you're building the right thing

### Between Sessions

**Observation Homework:**
- Ask her to track time spent on admin tasks for a week
- Ask her to note every time she thinks "this is annoying"
- Review her Excel sheet and invoices together
- Watch her create one invoice from start to finish

## Analysis Framework

After gathering insights, organize them:

### 1. User Persona Summary
- **Goals:** What is she trying to achieve?
- **Frustrations:** What blocks her from those goals?
- **Behaviors:** How does she currently work?
- **Motivations:** What drives her decisions?

### 2. Problem Statement Template

Fill in this template based on your findings:

```
[Your wife's name] needs a way to [do something]
because [insight about user/context]
but [current barrier/pain point].

Example:
"Sarah needs a way to track sessions and generate invoices quickly
because she's spending 6+ hours per month on manual admin work
but her Excel sheet requires too many manual calculations and is error-prone."
```

### 3. Jobs-to-be-Done Framework

For each main task, document:
- **Functional job:** What task is she trying to complete?
- **Emotional job:** How does she want to feel?
- **Social job:** How does she want to be perceived?

Example:
| Job | Current Solution | Pain Points | Opportunity |
|-----|------------------|-------------|-------------|
| Track sessions | Excel sheet | Manual entry, time-consuming | Auto-logging with quick capture |
| Generate invoices | Manual calculation | Error-prone, takes hours | One-click generation with email |
| Analyze student work | Upload to ChatGPT | Extra steps, context lost | Integrated AI with history |

### 4. Feature Prioritization Matrix

Plot features on this matrix:

```
High Impact, Low Effort → DO FIRST
High Impact, High Effort → DO NEXT
Low Impact, Low Effort → DO LATER
Low Impact, High Effort → DON'T DO
```

## Red Flags to Watch For

During your interviews, watch for these warning signs:

1. **Feature Creep**
   - She wants "everything" → Help narrow down to essentials
   - Ask: "If you could only have ONE feature, which would it be?"

2. **Solution Fixation**
   - She describes HOW it should work, not WHAT problem needs solving
   - Redirect: "Help me understand what you're trying to accomplish"

3. **Hypothetical Needs**
   - "I might want to..." or "It would be nice if..."
   - Ask: "Have you ever needed this in the past 6 months?"

4. **Change Resistance**
   - "I'm used to Excel" might mean the solution is too complex
   - Ask: "What would make switching worth it?"

## Sample Interview Script

Here's a complete example to get started:

---

**Introduction (2 min)**
"Hey, I want to understand your tutoring workflow better so I can help make your life easier. I'm not here to sell you on anything - I just want to listen and understand. There are no wrong answers, and I'd love to hear about frustrations and what works well. Sound good?"

**Warm-up (5 min)**
"Tell me about your favorite student right now. What makes teaching them enjoyable?"
[Build rapport, get her talking]

**Current State (15 min)**
"Walk me through what you did yesterday from a business admin perspective. Like, after teaching, what did you need to do?"
[Listen, take notes, ask follow-ups]

"Show me your Excel sheet. How do you use it?"
[Observe, don't critique]

**Pain Points (15 min)**
"What part of managing your students feels like a chore?"
"Tell me about the last time you created invoices. How did that go?"
"If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about your admin work, what would it be?"

**Future State (10 min)**
"Imagine it's 6 months from now and managing your tutoring business is effortless. What changed?"
"What would your ideal system do for you?"

**Wrap-up (5 min)**
"Of everything we discussed, what's the one thing you wish would get better ASAP?"
"Is there anything I didn't ask about that I should know?"

## Next Steps After Interview

1. **Synthesize findings** into key themes
2. **Create a problem statement** that resonates with her
3. **Sketch solution concepts** (show, don't tell)
4. **Validate with her** before building
5. **Build MVP** with only the essentials
6. **Test with her** and iterate

---

## Key Insights to Capture

As you interview, look for answers to:

- ✅ What is her biggest time sink?
- ✅ What causes her the most stress?
- ✅ What would she pay money to fix?
- ✅ What must work flawlessly vs. what's nice-to-have?
- ✅ How tech-savvy is she? (This affects UI/UX decisions)
- ✅ What devices does she use most?
- ✅ What's her tolerance for learning new tools?
- ✅ Does she want automation or control?

---

## Remember

**The goal isn't to validate your solution.**
**The goal is to understand the problem so well that the right solution becomes obvious.**
